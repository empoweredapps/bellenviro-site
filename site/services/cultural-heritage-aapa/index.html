Sacred site clearances (AAPA certificates) and cultural heritage

Sacred sites law applies to every bit of ground in the Territory, whatever the tenure. Most government and Defence contracts want an AAPA certificate before you break ground, and the certificate takes months. Bell Enviro gets the AAPA application in early, handles the heritage due diligence and the consultation, and brings in archaeologists or anthropologists when a job needs them.

What is included

  • AAPA Authority Certificate applications and clearance coordination
  • Heritage due diligence under the NT Heritage Act 2011
  • Traditional Owner and land council engagement, with consultation records
  • Archaeological surveys through qualified archaeologists
  • Heritage management plans and unexpected finds procedures for your CEMP

Who it is for

Civil contractors and developers on Crown land, Aboriginal land, remote community projects and greenfield sites anywhere in the NT.

Pricing

Scoped per project. AAPA fees and specialist fees at cost.

Available in Darwin, Palmerston & Rural · Top End · NT & Kimberley.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need an AAPA certificate?

It isn't compulsory, but it's the only legal defence if works damage a sacred site, and most NT Government and Defence contracts require one before ground disturbance.

How long does an AAPA certificate take?

Months. Which is why Bell Enviro raises it in the first conversation about any project.

Do you run the consultation yourselves?

Consultation goes through AAPA and the land councils. Bell Enviro coordinates it and engages qualified anthropologists and archaeologists when the project needs a specialist survey.
