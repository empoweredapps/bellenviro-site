PFAS testing for civil works around Darwin: what contractors need to know

By Chris Bell, Principal, Bell Enviro · Updated 2026-11-07

PFAS is the firefighting foam chemical. It's in the soil and groundwater around airports, Defence bases and fire training grounds, and around Darwin the declared investigation areas take in RAAF Base Darwin, Darwin International Airport and the suburbs and drains downstream. Old industrial sites and landfills can have it too, and so can old fire stations and public spaces where foam was used for events and demonstrations.

When excavated material needs testing

  • You're working inside or downstream of a declared PFAS investigation or management area.
  • Material is leaving site and the receiving facility wants PFAS results, which is now standard.
  • You're dewatering to stormwater or land.
  • The contract or the CEMP says so.

What the PFAS NEMP means on site

The PFAS National Environmental Management Plan sets the criteria for reusing, managing and disposing of PFAS-impacted soil and water. For a Top End contractor it comes down to this: test before you move it, keep it on site under a management plan where you can because licensed PFAS disposal in the NT is scarce, and don't spread impacted groundwater around with the dewatering.

Sampling matters

PFAS is in waterproof clothing, sunscreen, some sample bottles and plenty of ordinary field gear. If the sampling isn't done with PFAS-free equipment, the results are useless. Use someone who runs NEMP protocols and NATA labs.
