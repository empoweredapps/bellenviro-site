Gamba grass and weed management plans for Darwin rural blocks and civil sites

By Chris Bell, Principal, Bell Enviro · Updated 2026-11-06

Gamba grass is a declared weed with its own statutory Weed Management Plan, and that plan puts obligations on every landholder in the declared zone, which takes in Darwin, Palmerston, Litchfield and Coomalie. It burns up to eight times hotter than native grass and is the main reason Top End fire seasons keep getting worse.

What landholders have to do

  • Don't spread it. No moving gamba seed or plants, including in soil, hay, mulch, or on vehicles and machinery.
  • Control it on your block, with specific buffer requirements around buildings and boundaries depending on which zone you're in.
  • Keep records where the plan asks for them.

What it means for civil contractors

Plant leaving gamba-infested sites has to be cleaned, and most NTG and council contracts want weed hygiene declarations and a weed section in the CEMP. Topsoil and fill out of the Darwin rural area is a known way of getting gamba and mission grass into new subdivisions and remote communities.

What goes in a weed management plan

  • A map of what's where, by species and density.
  • Priorities: new outbreaks first, then containing the established areas, then the buffers.
  • Control methods and timing: spray at the right growth stage early in the Wet, slash before seed set, follow up.
  • Hygiene for vehicles, machinery and materials.
  • A monitoring and record schedule.
