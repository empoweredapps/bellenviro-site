Gamba grass and weed management plans for Darwin rural properties and civil sites

By Chris Bell, Principal, Bell Enviro · Updated 2026-11-05

Gamba grass (Andropogon gayanus) is a Class A/C declared weed in the Northern Territory with a statutory Weed Management Plan that imposes obligations on every landholder in the declared zone, which covers the Darwin, Palmerston, Litchfield and Coomalie areas. It fuels fires up to eight times more intense than native grass fires and is the main driver of the Top End's worsening fire seasons.

Landholder obligations

  • Prevent spread: no moving gamba seed or plants, including in soil, hay, mulch or on vehicles and machinery.
  • Control gamba on your property, with specific requirements for a cleared buffer around buildings and boundaries in the eradication and management zones.
  • Keep records of control where a plan requires them.

What this means for civil contractors

Plant mobilising from gamba-affected sites must be cleaned, and most NT Government and council contracts require weed hygiene declarations and a weed management section in the CEMP. Topsoil and fill from the Darwin rural area is a known pathway for spreading gamba and mission grass onto new sites and into remote communities.

What a property or project weed management plan contains

  • A map of infestations by species and density.
  • Priorities: new outbreaks first, then containment of established areas, then buffers.
  • Control methods and timing: spraying at the right growth stage (early Wet), slashing before seed set, and follow-up.
  • Hygiene procedures for vehicles, machinery and materials.
  • A monitoring and record schedule.
