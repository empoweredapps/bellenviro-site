AAPA Authority Certificates: what they are, when you need one and how long they take

By Chris Bell, Principal, Bell Enviro · Updated 2026-11-06

An Authority Certificate from the Aboriginal Areas Protection Authority, under the NT Aboriginal Sacred Sites Act 1989, sets out the conditions for doing work without damaging or interfering with sacred sites. It's the only legal defence against prosecution under the Act. The Act applies everywhere in the Territory, on any tenure, whether or not a site is registered.

When you need one

  • Most NT Government, Defence, land council and major private contracts require a current certificate before ground disturbance.
  • Any project on Aboriginal land, near a registered or recorded site, or in a greenfield area.
  • Any job where a stop-work order would hurt.

The process

  1. Lodge an application with AAPA describing the works and the exact area, with a map and the activities.
  2. AAPA consults the custodians, sometimes with a field survey.
  3. AAPA issues the certificate with conditions: restricted areas, buffers, reporting.

Timing and cost

Months. AAPA charges on the size and complexity of the area, and consultation and surveys add time and cost on remote jobs. Applying early with a clear, accurately mapped scope is the single biggest thing you can do to speed it up.
