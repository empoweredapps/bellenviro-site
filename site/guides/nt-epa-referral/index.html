NT EPA referral: when a project needs one and how the process works

By Chris Bell, Principal, Bell Enviro · Updated 2026-11-05

Under the NT Environment Protection Act 2019, a proponent must refer a proposed action to the NT EPA if it has the potential to have a significant impact on the environment. Referral is a decision the proponent makes, which is why so many projects get it wrong in both directions: referring when it is not needed and losing months, or not referring and discovering the problem at the approvals stage.

Does your project need to be referred?

  • Start with the NT EPA pre-referral screening tool and the Environmental Factors and Objectives guideline. Both are published on the NT EPA website.
  • Projects that commonly need referral: new or expanded extractive operations, large-scale land clearing, industrial facilities with significant discharges, major infrastructure in sensitive areas, and anything already flagged by another approval pathway (for example a mining authorisation or a water licence).
  • Projects that commonly do not: routine civil works within existing corridors, maintenance, subdivision earthworks already covered by a development permit, and small clearing within the permit thresholds.

What a referral contains

A referral describes the proposal, the receiving environment, the potential impacts against each of the NT EPA's environmental factors, and the proposed management measures. The quality of the referral determines whether the EPA decides no further assessment is required, which is the outcome most proponents want.

Timing

The EPA has a statutory period of around four to six weeks to decide on a referral once it is accepted as complete. If assessment is required, the process runs many months. Allow for both scenarios in the program.
