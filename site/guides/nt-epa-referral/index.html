NT EPA referral: when a project needs one and how it works

By Chris Bell, Principal, Bell Enviro · Updated 2026-11-06

Under the NT Environment Protection Act 2019, a proponent has to refer a proposal to the NT EPA if it could have a significant impact on the environment. It's the proponent's call, which is why projects get it wrong both ways: referring when they didn't need to and losing months, or not referring and finding out at the approvals stage.

Does your project need referring?

  • Start with the NT EPA's pre-referral screening tool and the Environmental Factors and Objectives guideline, both on the NT EPA website.
  • Usually yes: new or expanded extractive operations, large-scale clearing, industrial sites with significant discharges, major infrastructure in sensitive areas, and anything already flagged by another approval (a mining authorisation, a water licence).
  • Usually no: routine civil works in existing corridors, maintenance, subdivision earthworks already covered by a development permit, and small clearing under the permit thresholds.

What goes in a referral

The proposal, the receiving environment, the potential impacts against each of the EPA's environmental factors, and how you'll manage them. A good referral is what gets you the outcome most proponents want: a decision that no further assessment is required.

Timing

The EPA has roughly four to six weeks to decide on a referral once it's accepted as complete. If they decide to assess, it runs many months. Put both scenarios in the program.
