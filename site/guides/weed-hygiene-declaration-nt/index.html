Weed hygiene declarations for NT civil works: what is required

By Chris Bell, Principal, Bell Enviro · Updated 2026-11-05

The NT Weeds Management Act 2001 places a duty on everyone to prevent the spread of declared weeds, and NT Government, Defence, land council and many private contracts turn that duty into a documented requirement: a weed hygiene declaration for plant and vehicles entering site, and a weed management procedure in the CEMP.

What a weed hygiene declaration covers

  • Identification of the machine or vehicle and where it has come from.
  • Confirmation that it has been cleaned (wash-down or blow-down) to remove soil, seed and plant material, with the location and date.
  • Inspection by a competent person and sign-off.

Where it bites

Mobilising plant to Arnhem Land, the Tiwi Islands, Kakadu, Groote Eylandt and pastoral properties often requires declarations and inspections before the barge or at the property boundary. Gamba grass, mission grass and mimosa are the high-profile risks in the Top End; the Kimberley adds its own declared species.

Setting it up

A workable procedure needs a declaration form, a wash-down location and method, an inspection checklist, a register of declarations, and a toolbox talk so operators know why it matters. Bell Enviro provides these as part of CEMPs and can conduct pre-mobilisation inspections. See CEMPs and ESCPs.
