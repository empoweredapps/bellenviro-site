Weed hygiene declarations for NT civil works: what's required

By Chris Bell, Principal, Bell Enviro · Updated 2026-11-06

The NT Weeds Management Act 2001 puts a duty on everyone to stop declared weeds spreading. NT Government, Defence, land council and a lot of private contracts turn that into paperwork: a weed hygiene declaration for every bit of plant and every vehicle coming onto site, and a weed section in the CEMP.

What a declaration covers

  • Which machine or vehicle it is and where it has come from.
  • That it has been cleaned (wash-down or blow-down) of soil, seed and plant material, where and when.
  • Inspection and sign-off by someone competent.

Where it bites

Taking plant into Arnhem Land, the Tiwi Islands, Kakadu, Groote Eylandt or onto pastoral stations often means declarations and inspections before the barge or at the boundary. Gamba grass, mission grass and mimosa are the big ones in the Top End. The Kimberley has its own list.

Setting it up

You need a declaration form, somewhere and some way to wash down, an inspection checklist, a register of declarations, and a toolbox talk so the operators know why they're doing it. Bell Enviro provides all of this as part of a CEMP and can do pre-mobilisation inspections. See CEMPs and ESCPs.
