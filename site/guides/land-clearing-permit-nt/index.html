Land clearing permits in the NT: when you need one and how to apply

By Chris Bell, Principal, Bell Enviro · Updated 2026-11-05

Clearing native vegetation in the Northern Territory generally requires a permit from the Development Consent Authority under the Planning Act 1999 and the NT Planning Scheme, assessed against the NT Land Clearing Guidelines. On pastoral land, a separate pastoral land clearing process applies through the Pastoral Land Board.

When a permit is needed

  • Clearing more than the exempt threshold on a freehold lot (the threshold depends on the zone; in the rural area it is small).
  • Clearing within buffers of watercourses, wetlands, sensitive vegetation such as rainforest and monsoon vine thicket, or habitat of threatened species.
  • Clearing for subdivision, horticulture, industrial development or new roads and tracks.

Exemptions that commonly apply

Fire breaks of prescribed width, fence lines, clearing within a defined distance of a dwelling, and maintenance of previously cleared land. Exemptions are specific; confirm them against the current guidelines before relying on one.

What an application needs

  • A clearing plan on aerial imagery showing the area, retained vegetation and buffers.
  • A vegetation and habitat description, including threatened species and sensitive vegetation checks.
  • Erosion and sediment control, weed management and drainage measures.
  • Justification for the extent of clearing and consideration of alternatives.
