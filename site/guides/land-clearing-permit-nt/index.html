Land clearing permits in the NT: when you need one and how to apply

By Chris Bell, Principal, Bell Enviro · Updated 2026-11-06

Clearing native vegetation in the NT generally needs a permit from the Development Consent Authority under the Planning Act 1999 and the NT Planning Scheme, assessed against the NT Land Clearing Guidelines. Pastoral land has its own process through the Pastoral Land Board.

When you need a permit

  • Clearing more than the exempt area on a freehold lot. The threshold depends on the zone and in the rural area it's small.
  • Clearing within the buffers of watercourses, wetlands, rainforest and monsoon vine thicket, or threatened species habitat.
  • Clearing for subdivision, horticulture, industrial development, or new roads and tracks.

Exemptions that commonly apply

Fire breaks of the prescribed width, fence lines, clearing within a set distance of a dwelling, and maintaining land that was already cleared. They're specific. Check the current guidelines before relying on one.

What goes in an application

  • A clearing plan on aerial imagery showing the area, what's retained and the buffers.
  • A description of the vegetation and habitat, including threatened species and sensitive vegetation checks.
  • Erosion and sediment control, weed management and drainage.
  • Why you need that much clearing and what else was considered.
