Do I need a CEMP for NT Government or Defence civil works?

By Chris Bell, Principal, Bell Enviro · Updated 2026-11-07

Short answer: almost certainly. NT Government (DIPL, Power and Water, Housing), Defence estate and council construction contracts nearly all have a clause requiring a CEMP before site establishment. Private principals are asking for them too, because their own approval conditions say so.

What triggers it

  • A contract clause, usually in the general conditions or the environmental section of the spec, requiring an approved CEMP before you start.
  • A development permit or NT EPA approval condition that gets passed down to the contractor.
  • Work near waterways, on Aboriginal land, in Kakadu, on Defence land, or on a site with known contamination.

What the superintendent wants to see

  • Risks specific to this site, not a generic table: Wet-season erosion, dust, weeds, fuel and chemical storage, waste and stockpiles, heritage, fauna.
  • Controls that match your actual methodology and plant.
  • An Erosion and Sediment Control Plan, normally with a drawing.
  • Weed hygiene procedures and vehicle wash-down declarations.
  • Monitoring, inspection and incident procedures with names against them.
  • Evidence of inductions and training.

Getting it approved first time

Send your consultant the full environmental clauses, the spec, the approval conditions and your methodology. Plans get rejected when they are generic and don't reference the contract. A plan written from the contract normally gets through in one round.
