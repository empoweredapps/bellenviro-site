Do I need a CEMP for NT Government or Defence civil works?

By Chris Bell, Principal, Bell Enviro · Updated 2026-11-03

Almost all NT Government (DIPL, Power and Water, Department of Housing), Defence estate and local council construction contracts include an environmental management clause requiring the contractor to prepare and implement a Construction Environmental Management Plan before site establishment. Private principals increasingly require one too, because their own approvals say so.

What triggers the requirement

  • A contract clause (commonly in the general conditions or the specification's environmental section) requiring an approved CEMP before commencement.
  • A development permit or NT EPA approval condition that is passed down to the contractor.
  • Works on or near waterways, Aboriginal land, Kakadu, Defence land or sites with known contamination.

What a superintendent expects to see

  • Site-specific environmental risks (not a generic risk table), including Wet-season erosion, dust, weeds, fuel and chemical storage, waste and stockpiles, heritage and fauna.
  • Controls tied to the contractor's actual methodology and plant.
  • An Erosion and Sediment Control Plan, usually with a drawing.
  • Weed hygiene procedures and vehicle wash-down declarations.
  • Monitoring, inspection and incident reporting procedures with named responsibilities.
  • Evidence of inductions and training.

How to get it approved first time

Send the environmental consultant the full contract environmental clauses, the specification, the approval conditions and your methodology. Plans rejected by superintendents are almost always generic ones that do not reference the contract. A plan built from the contract is typically approved in one round.
