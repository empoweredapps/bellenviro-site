PSI vs DSI: which contaminated land assessment do you need in the NT?

By Chris Bell, Principal, Bell Enviro · Updated 2026-11-06

Contaminated land assessment in the NT follows the National Environment Protection (Assessment of Site Contamination) Measure 1999, the ASC NEPM, which the NT EPA adopts as its standard. It's a staged process, and you only go to the next stage if the current one says you need to.

Preliminary Site Investigation (PSI)

A PSI answers one question: is contamination likely here? It looks at land titles, historical aerial photos, government records, old reports and whoever knows the site, then walks the ground looking for evidence. The result is a conceptual site model and a yes or no on further work. Sometimes a few samples go in at this stage if they'll settle the question early.

Typical triggers: buying land, a DA where the planning authority asks about contamination, rezoning to a more sensitive use, and pulling out fuel storage.

Detailed Site Investigation (DSI)

A DSI goes and samples. Soil, groundwater, sometimes vapour, designed from the PSI's site model under a sampling and analysis quality plan and reported against the NEPM investigation levels. If it finds a risk, the next step is a remediation action plan or a site management plan.

Which one?

  • No history of anything nasty and a planning authority asking a general question: PSI.
  • Known or suspected contamination, a PSI that recommended more work, or an auditor involved: DSI.
  • Something unexpected in a trench (staining, smell, drums, fill of unknown origin): a targeted investigation and waste classification, usually in a hurry.
