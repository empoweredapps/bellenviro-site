PSI vs DSI: which contaminated land assessment do you need in the NT?

By Chris Bell, Principal, Bell Enviro · Updated 2026-11-03

Contaminated land assessment in the Northern Territory follows the National Environment Protection (Assessment of Site Contamination) Measure 1999 (ASC NEPM), which NT EPA adopts as its standard. The NEPM sets out a staged approach.

Preliminary Site Investigation (PSI)

A PSI answers one question: is contamination plausible at this site? It reviews land titles and historical aerial photographs, government records, previous reports and interviews, then walks the site to look for evidence. The output is a conceptual site model and a conclusion on whether further investigation is needed. A PSI may include limited sampling where it helps answer the question early.

Typical triggers: land purchase due diligence, development applications where the planning authority asks about contamination, rezoning to a more sensitive use, and decommissioning of fuel storage.

Detailed Site Investigation (DSI)

A DSI collects and analyses soil, groundwater and sometimes vapour samples to confirm whether contamination exists, how far it extends and whether it poses a risk to the intended land use. It is designed from the PSI's conceptual site model using a sampling and analysis quality plan, and reports against NEPM investigation levels. Where risk is identified, it leads to a remediation action plan or site management plan.

Which one do you need?

  • No history of contaminating activity and a planning authority asking a general question: PSI.
  • Known or suspected contamination, a PSI recommending further work, or an auditor involved: DSI.
  • Unexpected finds during civil works (staining, odours, buried drums, fill of unknown origin): targeted investigation and waste classification, often expedited.
